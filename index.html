01 OUR STORY

A farm with room
for nature.

On Buvuma Island, Eco Granja brings together productive farming and a deep appreciation for the natural landscape.

Goat breeding is a major part of Eco Granja, alongside cocoa, Hass avocado, and beekeeping. Each activity contributes to a broader agricultural story rooted in the island’s landscape. Alongside these activities, areas of natural vegetation remain untouched — valued as part of the living landscape, not simply land waiting to be used.

03 NATURE & CONSERVATION

Not every space
needs to be cultivated.

Some areas are valuable just as they are. Eco Granja retains untouched green coverage as part of the farm's natural landscape — making room for existing vegetation and a relationship with the land that reaches beyond production.

“Growing well means recognising what is worth leaving alone.”

04 PRACTICAL FARM NOTES

Small cycles. Lasting value.

Simple ways to make better use of what the farm already has, while caring for soil, water, animals, and surrounding vegetation.

✳01 / GOAT MANURE

Turn manure into a resource

Goat manure and used bedding can be composted with dry leaves and other plant material. Allow the mixture to break down fully before applying it to soil.

↻02 / SOIL HEALTH

Feed the soil, not just the crop

Use well-matured compost to return organic matter to the soil. Apply it thoughtfully around crops and keep it away from streams and the lake edge.

⌁03 / HEALTHY HERD

Manage grazing with care

Where practical, rotate grazing areas and give vegetation time to recover. Keep animals supplied with clean water and provide appropriate shelter.

◌04 / NATURAL BALANCE

Protect the living landscape

Keep useful ground cover, protect existing trees, and leave sensitive natural areas undisturbed where possible. Healthy ecosystems are part of a resilient farm.

Farm note: fresh manure can harm plants and may carry pathogens. Compost it properly, follow local agricultural guidance, and wash hands after handling manure.

06 PEOPLE & COMMUNITY

Behind every growing farm
are people.

Eco Granja’s story is also a story of the people who show up, care for the land, and help keep the farm moving.

Our foreman helps look after the farm while raising a family of ten children. His story is a reminder that agriculture is about more than harvests: it is about livelihoods, family, dignity, and the opportunities that can grow when a community is supported.

As Eco Granja develops, we want its progress to reflect respect for the people behind the work and a thoughtful commitment to the wider Buvuma community.

Our recent community visit in Buvuma brought us together with local children and families. We are sharing this part of the journey to encourage practical acts of care and support for the community, while respecting the privacy and dignity of the people pictured.

Community photographs are selected or cropped to protect privacy and focus on people’s work, learning spaces, and place.
